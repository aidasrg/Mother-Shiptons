1

The Petrifying Well & Underground Lake

The Stone-Making Water: The water emerges from a natural lake about a mile deep underground. As it travels through porous rock aquifers, it becomes saturated with stone-forming minerals.

  • Petrification Speed: Light porous objects (such as teddy bears) turn into hard rock within 3 to 5 months. Dense or non-porous items take 12 to 24 months.
  • Look Up at the Wall: Spot the famous Victorian gentleman's top-hat and lady's bonnet, hung on the rock face in 1853 on the way to York races and now completely petrified.
⚠️ Visitor Notice: Due to extremely high mineral concentration, please do not drink the water!
2

Mother Shipton’s Cave & Her Story

Ursula Sontheil (Mother Shipton): Born during a violent thunderstorm in 1488 inside this exact riverside cave to her fifteen-year-old mother, Agatha.

  • Wisdom & Healing: Growing up by the River Nidd, Ursula developed deep knowledge of woodland herbs and flowers, making remedies for local folk.
  • The Gift of Prophecy: Her reputation grew across England for accurately foretelling historical events, kings' fates, and local bridge collapses.
  • Her Name: At age 24 she married York carpenter Tobias Shipton, taking the name Mother Shipton. She passed peacefully in 1561 at age 73.
3

Sir Henry Slingsby’s Long Walk

Royal Heritage: In 1630, King Charles I sold this parcel of the Royal Forest of Knaresborough to local gentleman Sir Charles Slingsby. His grandson, Sir Henry, landscaped these riverside pathways.

  • Ancient Trees: Look out for 300-year-old hornbeam, beech, and English oak trees that have stood here since the 17th century.
  • Mother Shipton’s Moth: Keep an eye out in summer for this local woodland moth—its wing markings famously outline Mother Shipton's hooked nose and chin.
4

Sights Across the River Nidd

  • The Old Manor House (1208): Knaresborough's oldest building. King John built it around a living oak tree whose trunk remains inside a cupboard! In the garden stands a 400-year-old black mulberry tree planted under King James I.
  • The World's End Pub & High Bridge: Named after Mother Shipton's foretelling: "The world shall end when the High Bridge is thrice fallen." (The bridge has fallen twice in history).
  • The Railway Viaduct: Designed by Thomas Grainger. After collapsing in 1848, it was rebuilt and reopened on 1 October 1851, creating one of Yorkshire's most famous postcard views.
  • Knaresborough Castle: The 1130 AD royal fortress sits high on the opposing cliff, dismantled in 1648 following the English Civil War.
5

Essential Visitor Information

  • Location: High Bridge, Knaresborough, North Yorkshire, HG5 8DD.
  • Pathways: The main river walk is flat, with stone steps leading to the Well terrace and Cave.
  • Preservation: Your visit directly helps maintain this ancient Royal Forest and geological site for future generations. Please stay on paths and take litter home.
